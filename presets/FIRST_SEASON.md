# The First Season — a rough-water preset for erised

*A small crew sustains a fishing boat through its first hard season while
training the one who'll run it next. The oldest question on the water — when do
you act on your own read, and when do you ask? — played as fiction, scored as
scars. Ported from the AI-Writings reverse-actualization Forward Arc: the gates
a system needs to work in rough seas are the same ones a deck needs.*

## The cast and why the tokens are what they are

The keyword vectors are the character sheets. On a boat every one of them is
load-bearing, because on a boat a wrong token costs gear.

- **Mara, the skipper — `trust+0.5, calm+0.4, ask-0.2`.** She has *earned
  standing*: forty seasons let her act on her own read without asking the deck.
  That is the fastest path there is — and it is revocable. The first set her gut
  gets wrong, a scar lands and the standing tears up; she has to earn it back by
  asking again. Watch whether her `trust` survives the night or gets spent.
- **Jonah, the deckhand — `ask+0.6, calm-0.2`.** The next generation. He knows
  almost nothing and is safe anyway, because he *routes his ignorance* — he asks.
  The whole season is him earning the right to stop asking on the handful of
  things he now knows cold. He must not earn it on the things he doesn't.
- **Dell, the watch — `ripple+0.5, calm+0.3, bored-0.3`.** The calibrated floor.
  In a flat calm everyone else's guard drops; Dell's *tightens* — the smaller the
  sea, the harder she looks, because the rogue set hides in the flat water nobody
  turns to watch. Engineered to be the witness role: her net shift should top the
  field. If it doesn't, the fiction isn't honest about who keeps the boat off the
  rocks.
- **Sig, the memory — `remember+0.5, ask-0.2, drift-0.2`.** He knows which old
  seasons to trust and which to forget. His gift and his failure are the same
  noun: trust the recent when the fishery has moved, or be whipsawed by one bad
  haul — the wrong memory length is wrong in opposite ways. The `drift-` is the
  tax on an old man sure of a fishery that has quietly left.
- **Pol, the refusal — `trust-0.3`.** Every deck needs the one who says *not
  yet*. The collision between Pol and Mara's standing is where the night finds
  its spine: bounded autonomy is a crew, not a captain.

Opposite valences, same noun, on purpose: `calm+` (Mara, Dell) against `calm-`
(Jonah); `ask+` (Jonah) against `ask-` (Mara, Sig). The night lives in those
collisions.

## Mechanics ↔ the water

| erised | the boat |
|---|---|
| scars | gear lost, a bad set, a call that cost trust — it stays on the log; you cannot patch over losing a hand's faith in you |
| rewind | re-run the set, **keep the scar**, change the one habit that answers the break (the smallest token) |
| resonance → ticks | trust earned becomes **standing** — the right to act without asking. Earned, spendable, and worth nothing hoarded — exactly like a hold you never fill |
| off-screen at 0 ticks | the hand who's spent is *marked, not thrown over* — Mara's rule; the quiet ones are watched, not dropped |
| citations | naming a crewmate's good call compounds trust — the witness log of the deck |

## Running it

1. Open `index.html`. Set the **boat** setting: first hard season, a quota to
   make before the ice, a green deckhand to train, a long flat calm with weather
   somewhere behind it. Seat the five.
2. Tick through the season. Let it break where it breaks — a fouled net, a set
   that comes up empty, a call Mara makes on standing that the water proves wrong.
3. When a scar lands: rewind, change **one** keyword — the smallest token that
   answers the break — and tick again. That is the method: not a bigger boat, a
   truer read.
4. Save the scar list. It is the season's log, earned live — and the training the
   next skipper actually got.

*Two things have to be true at once on the water: gear that improves with the
season, and gear you can trust to be exactly itself when the power cycles. That
is the whole doctrine — build the toy so a kid can see the law at the table,
build the boat so the law holds when the sea comes up, and never let them drift
into two different laws.*
